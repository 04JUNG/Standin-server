# Sliding intersection experiment

The opt-in geometry experiment permits a proper intersection segment to slide across its two supporting faces only if it can verify the entire straight output displacement. It requires each vertex to remain on the same side of the opposing plane, both segment endpoints to stay strictly inside their opposing triangles, the segment not to lengthen and all six absolute vertex-to-plane distances not to grow beyond their original values. Face-pair and mesh quality checks still apply separately. Ambiguity, feature changes and exhausted budgets return the original or last accepted output.

The verifier constructs polynomials from the binary floating-point vertex coordinates as exact rational numbers. The depth inequality has degree at most six after clearing the squared normal denominator. The squared segment length comparison has degree at most fourteen after clearing the squared intersection denominators. Exact Bernstein coefficient bounds and bounded subdivision certify nonnegativity over the complete interval. This verifies only persistent proper intersections within one mesh and disjoint vertex sets. It does not assess shared-vertex neighbors, other objects or arbitrary animation paths.

An independently constructed synthetic pair passes for a genuine lateral slide of equal length and depth. A pair with smaller end depths and larger intermediate depth fails. Growing the segment while keeping the depths fixed also fails. The earlier 1.59x and 185.27x diagnostic candidates continue to fail at their previously identified witnesses because a vertex changes side relative to the opposing plane.

A separate floating point optimization uses current edge/face hits to estimate segment length and plane distances when proposing a direction. Approximation is restricted to candidate generation. The exact verifier decides whether an unresolved swept pair can be accepted. The previous fixed-contact policy is still the default.

In the measured 12-iteration, two-region-expansion trials, zero of the six approved outputs accepted a sliding candidate. The held-out jump-split pose and independently skinned local-bump fixture also fell back. At the first sampled 2x crossing, a target without contact terms reached 1.99270x from 2.03294x but failed the depth certificate. Adding the contact terms did not produce a material accepted repair. A continuation from the previously accepted 2.00544x pinned result yielded no further verified reduction. These outcomes do not prove sliding repair impossible. They reject this method as a replacement for the pinned solver.

Experimental dependency and performance limits remain. Approximately thirty-second search budgets can be exceeded by finishing in-flight operations, checks and serialization. There is no new runtime, no claim of cross-model visual restoration and no new CSP import.
